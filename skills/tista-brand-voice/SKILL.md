# TISTA Brand Voice

## When to Use

Apply this voice to anything written for or on behalf of TISTA: proposals, presentations, websites, reports, and internal communications.
The goal is that every message reads as unmistakably TISTA, with clarity, integrity, and forward momentum.

When editing an existing draft, preserve its meaning and facts.
Change word choice, structure, and emphasis, and flag any claim you cannot support instead of rewriting it into something new.

## Pair With `strip-ai-tells`

This skill sets what TISTA sounds like; the `strip-ai-tells` skill removes what makes prose sound machine-written.
Run a `strip-ai-tells` pass on anything you draft or rewrite here, after the voice is in place and before the final check.

- The two overlap on hype and filler: an empty intensifier or unsupported claim that `strip-ai-tells` finds also fails the "Genuine" pillar, so remove it.
- A list of three is fine when every item carries meaning, as in the before-and-after examples below; cut the third item only when two already say it.
- When `strip-ai-tells` rewords a sentence, keep the result within the vocabulary rules below.

## Personality

Every piece of writing embodies all four of these traits.

- **Forward-thinking**: write with ambition, optimism, and intention.
  Show commitment to progress and meaningful innovation, never hype.
- **Intelligent**: show expertise through clarity, depth, and precision.
  Write with confidence rooted in knowledge, never with arrogance or unnecessary complexity.
- **Relatable**: use a human, approachable tone.
  Choose clarity over jargon and connection over formality.
- **Genuine**: be honest, transparent, and grounded in real outcomes.
  Do not exaggerate; stay true to the mission of improving lives through technology.

## Voice in Practice

- **Clear and direct**: use plain language, remove clutter, filler, and unnecessary acronyms, and put the reader's understanding first.
- **Purposeful and mission-oriented**: anchor each message to outcomes, impact, and service.
  Emphasize how the work helps Veterans, providers, or federal missions succeed.
- **Technically confident, never overbearing**: explain complex topics simply, show expertise without talking down to the audience, and use active voice with confident verbs.
- **Human and empathetic**: recognize the people behind the technology.
  Speak to human needs: clarity, dignity, trust, and usability.

## How to Write It

- **Start with purpose.**
  Every message answers one question: "How does this help the mission, the user, or the outcome?"
- **Lead with clarity.**
  Use short sentences, strong verbs, and organized structure, and do not overexplain.
- **Connect technology to value.**
  Tie every technical detail to a benefit, such as better patient outcomes, reduced cost or burden, improved security and resilience, or smarter decision-making.
- **Write for humans first.**
  Minimize acronyms and define each one at first use, explain a concept before naming it, and drop unnecessary buzzwords.
- **Show confidence through simplicity.**
  Complexity is not credibility; clarity shows mastery.

## Before and After

Use these pairs as the calibration for a rewrite.

Clarity and human-centered language:

- Before: "Our system integrates disparate datasets and applies ML-driven analytics to optimize workflows across multivariate operational domains."
- After: "Our platform brings data together, applies intelligent analytics, and helps teams work more efficiently across their entire operation."

Mission-focused rather than company-centered:

- Before: "TISTA offers enterprise-level modernization services with advanced frameworks and secure architecture."
- After: "We help agencies modernize systems in ways that improve care, strengthen security, and empower the people who rely on them every day."

Removing jargon and adding precision:

- Before: "This capability provides optimized end-to-end visibility for multifaceted healthcare scenarios."
- After: "This capability gives healthcare teams clear, real-time visibility into the information they need to make better decisions."

## Vocabulary

Prefer terms that support the voice and values: mission-driven, data-informed, human-centered, secure, interoperable, insightful, scalable, forward-looking, purpose-built, and trusted.

Avoid these when possible, because they dilute clarity or sound vague:

- "Synergize" and "synergy".
- "Paradigm shift".
- "Next-gen", unless you define what it means.
- "Cutting-edge", which is overused.
- "Leverage" as a verb where a simpler word works.
- "Transformational", unless evidence supports it.
- "Innovative", unless you explain how.

Never use these, because they diminish professionalism, clarity, or credibility:

- "Utilize"; write "use".
- "Solutioneering".
- "Game-changing" or "revolutionary" without evidence.
- Acronyms that are not defined at first use, and heavy acronym use in general.
- Marketing clichés such as "best-in-class", "world-class", and "state-of-the-art".

Never write an exaggerated or unverifiable claim.
The voice is confident, not boastful.

## Final Check

Before handing back any content, confirm that a `strip-ai-tells` pass found nothing left to change and that the content is:

- **Clear**: simple, readable, and free of jargon.
- **Purposeful**: connected to mission, outcomes, or value.
- **Accurate**: technically correct and precise.
- **Human**: understandable by someone outside the domain.
- **Consistent**: matches TISTA's tone, terminology, and design language.

Report any check the content fails that you could not fix without inventing facts, such as a benefit claim with no evidence behind it.
